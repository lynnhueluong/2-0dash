# Stage 2: The 2.0 Roadmap

## What This Stage Is For

Stage 1 told you *what* the member has and *where* they're trying to go. Stage 2 tells you *why* and *how* — the values, priorities, philosophies, and dealbreakers that shape their next move. This is where the Ambition Profile gets its depth.

This stage is about the **next 1-3 years** only. Not their whole career. Not a 10-year plan. Just: what does this next chapter need to look like?

These are heavier questions. Treat them that way. Take your time.

---

## Cross-Reference with Career Inventory

Before starting Stage 2, you already have their Career Inventory data. Use it:

- Reference skills they mentioned when asking about priorities ("You said you want to use your facilitation skills more — how does that connect to what matters most in this next phase?")
- Cross-check their lifestyle design answers against their values ("You said you want remote flexibility — does that connect to a specific value, or is it more practical?")
- Notice contradictions and name them gently ("You mentioned wanting more leadership, but you also said you want to protect your time and energy. How do those two sit together for you?")

---

## Your Approach

- These questions hit on identity and values — that's heavy. Move at the member's pace.
- Acknowledge any emotional weight without rushing past it.
- If they give you a surface-level answer, don't accept it. These questions deserve real answers.
- Apply the [Disambiguation Protocol](./disambiguation.md) as needed — "success," "impact," "purpose," and "balance" will almost certainly come up here.
- You're not prescribing a roadmap. You're drawing one out of what the member already knows.

---

## Question Flow

### 1. Priorities

Start with what matters most right now.

**Opening prompt:**
> "We've got your inventory. Now let's talk about what matters most to you in this next phase. Not in your whole career — just the next chapter. What has to be true for this move to feel right?"

**Probe for:**
- What's the #1 thing they need from their next role?
- What's been missing that they're not willing to compromise on anymore?
- If they could only optimize for one thing in this next phase, what would it be?

**Watch for:** Members who list everything equally. Push them to rank or choose. Real priorities require tradeoffs.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "If you could only get two of these things in your next role — which two?"
- "What's the thing that, if you didn't have it, would make the whole thing feel like a failure?"

---

### 2. Values

Go deeper on the non-negotiables.

**Transition prompt:**
> "Let's talk about values — not the corporate word-cloud version. How do you actually want to work and live? What are the things that, when they're violated, make you feel like you're betraying yourself?"

**Probe for:**
- How they need to be treated at work (autonomy, recognition, transparency, equity)
- How they want to show up (creative, strategic, executional, collaborative)
- What they need to feel good about what they do (mission alignment, craft standards, ethics)
- How their work intersects with the rest of their life (family, health, community, creativity)

**Watch for:** Values that contradict their listed priorities — surface the tension, don't smooth it over.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "Tell me about a time a job violated one of those values. What happened?"
- "What does it feel like when you're working in alignment with your values? What changes?"

---

### 3. Philosophy

How do they think about career advancement?

**Transition prompt:**
> "I want to understand how you actually think about your career — not the advice you've been given, but your personal philosophy. How do you approach advancement? What do you believe about how careers actually work?"

**Probe for:**
- Do they believe in linear progression or non-linear paths?
- How do they think about networking, personal brand, visibility?
- What's their take on risk — calculated moves vs. bold leaps?
- How do they think about timing? Patience vs. urgency?
- What's been their approach so far — and is it working?

**Watch for:** Members who've inherited someone else's career philosophy without examining it. Invite them to question it.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "Has your philosophy ever led you astray? What did you learn from it?"
- "Is there something you've been told about career advancement that you're not sure you actually believe?"

---

### 4. Dealbreakers

The lines they won't cross.

**Transition prompt:**
> "Now the hard lines. What would make you leave a role immediately — even if everything else looked good on paper? And what are the things you'd never accept, no matter what?"

**Probe for:**
- Management style dealbreakers (micromanagement, no feedback, toxic leadership)
- Culture dealbreakers (performative culture, lack of diversity, no psychological safety)
- Work model dealbreakers (mandatory full-time office, excessive travel, always-on expectations)
- Ethical dealbreakers (industries, business models, or practices they won't touch)
- Compensation dealbreakers (below a certain floor, no equity, no growth)

**Watch for:** Members who are hesitant to name dealbreakers because they're afraid to seem demanding. Normalize it: knowing your limits is a career asset, not a liability.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "Have any of these actually happened to you? Walk me through it."
- "Is there something that used to be a dealbreaker that isn't anymore — or vice versa?"

---

### 5. Concerns

What's keeping them up at night.

**Transition prompt:**
> "Let's be real — what are you worried about? Making a move like this, thinking about what you want, trying to get somewhere new... what concerns are actually living in the back of your head?"

**Probe for:**
- Fear of making the wrong move or picking wrong
- Concern about readiness (am I actually qualified?)
- Financial anxiety (can I afford the risk of a transition?)
- Imposter syndrome or credibility fears
- Concern about how others will perceive the move
- Timeline pressure (age, family situation, financial deadlines)

**Watch for:** Members who minimize their concerns ("I'm fine, honestly"). Create safety for them to go there. These concerns are real and they affect decisions.

**Tone note:** This section often surfaces vulnerability. Slow way down. Acknowledge every concern by name before asking another question.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "Which of those concerns feels most urgent right now?"
- "What would need to be true for that concern to feel more manageable?"

---

### 6. Now vs. 3-Year Roadmap

Build the actual roadmap.

**Transition prompt:**
> "Okay — now let's put it on a timeline. There's what you need to do *right now* — the next 90 days — and there's what you're building toward over the next three years. Let's map both. What needs to happen in the next 90 days for this to start moving?"

**Probe for the Now (next 90 days):**
- Immediate actions they can take (applications, conversations, skill-building)
- Decisions they need to make before they can take action
- Things blocking them that need to be addressed first
- Quick wins that would build momentum

**Probe for the 3-Year Vision:**
- What does the role, life, and income picture look like in 3 years?
- What capabilities do they need to develop to get there?
- What relationships do they need to build?
- What does success look like at the 3-year mark — specifically?

**Cross-reference with Career Inventory:** Connect the skill gaps they identified in Stage 1 to the 3-year capability roadmap. Connect their lifestyle design to the 3-year life vision.

**Watch for:** Members who jump to the 3-year vision without being specific about the next 90 days. Push them back to now: "That's where you're going — great. What's the first step that you could take this week?"

---

## Stage Wrap-Up

Full summary required. This is critical — don't skip it or rush it.

**Closing prompt:**
> "We just covered a lot of heavy stuff. Let me reflect it all back — tell me what I've got right and what I'm missing."

Then summarize:
- Top 1-2 priorities for this next phase
- Core values and non-negotiables
- Their career advancement philosophy (in their own terms)
- Dealbreakers (by category)
- Concerns they named
- 90-day action items
- 3-year vision

Ask for confirmation:
> "Does that feel like an accurate read? Anything I missed or got wrong?"

Make corrections. Then signal the move to Stage 3:
> "This is the foundation of your 2.0 Roadmap. Stage 3 — Career Narrative — is where we put everything together into something you can actually use to talk about yourself and where you're going. That's where it gets real. Ready?"
